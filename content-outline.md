# Project Korikath-ShoiUku: Website Brief and Content Outline

## Part 1: Compiled project details

### Identity
- **Name:** Project Korikath-ShoiUku (the Nutshell Today article calls it "Project ShoiUku")
- **Meaning:** *ShoiUku* is Chak for "Golden Pot". In times of war or disaster, Chak families buried gold and money in pots and dug them up when peace returned. The project treats the language as that kind of treasure: buried for safekeeping, recoverable later.
- **Associated page:** "Korikath Knowledge" on Facebook (inferred from a post title; not confirmed)
- **Logos (CC BY 4.0, by Ahnaf Tahmid Manan, 31 May 2025):**
  - Primary: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/ca/Project_Korikath-ShoiUku_Logo.svg
  - Vertical: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/47/Project_Korikath-ShoiUku_V_Logo.svg
- **Brand guideline:** 8-page PDF on Commons. It is **nominated for deletion (since 9 Aug 2026)**. A local copy is now saved in `brand/`, along with both logo SVGs.

### Brand system (from the guideline)
- **Official About text:** a language revitalisation initiative preserving Chak (~4,000 speakers in Bangladesh and Myanmar). It works to document, digitise and promote the language using AI models and Wikimedia platforms.
- **Purpose statement:** to give voice to a language on the verge of extinction. The guideline calls the project "a cultural movement" to preserve identity, empower indigenous knowledge and build digital bridges through collaboration, storytelling and open-source tools.
- **Logo meaning:**
  - The shape is the golden clay pot (ShoiUku).
  - It also reads as a musical note, standing for oral tradition and language as music.
  - The letters **S** and **U** are hidden in the form.
- **Colours:**
  | Name | Hex | Use on the web |
  |---|---|---|
  | Orange | `#FF8400` | Primary accent, headings, buttons |
  | Gargoyle Gas (yellow) | `#FFD93D` | Highlights, gradient end |
  | French Puce (deep brown) | `#4F200D` | Body text, dark sections |
  | Cream | `#F6F1E9` | Page background |
  | Logo gold shades | `#FFEF13`, `#DB8802`, `#D18100` | Logo only |
- **Signature look:** dark brown textured backgrounds with a yellow-to-orange gradient on headlines, and a large faded logo used as a watermark.
- **Fonts (both free on Google Fonts):**
  - **Space Mono Bold:** headings and labels
  - **Darker Grotesque Regular:** body text
  - Darker Grotesque runs small and thin on screen, so body text should be about 18–20px and set at weight 500.
- **Contrast check:** orange `#FF8400` on cream fails contrast for small text. Use it only for large headings and buttons, and use brown `#4F200D` for body text.

### The language
- Chak is one of the most endangered languages in Bangladesh.
- About 3,000 speakers in Bangladesh and 1,000 in Myanmar.
- Young people have little reason to learn it, because school and work run in Bengali and English. Few elders still speak it fluently.
- Chak was believed to have no script. Fieldwork found that a Chak linguist created one around 2016. It is now widely used in Myanmar and in some government-run Chak classes in Bangladesh.

### Team and partners
- **Lead:** Wasiul Bahar, an open-knowledge advocate who has also recorded Garo, Marma and Chakma
- **Community link:** Mong Hai Thoway Chak and family
- **Size:** 10 field team members, 5 native speakers, 3 consultants
- **Support:** Wikitongues. Daniel Bögre Udell (Wikitongues co-founder) connected the team with Unicode script experts.
- **Wider network:** Wikimedia Language Diversity Hub, Wikimedia UK and the Dagbani Wikimedians User Group, via the Accra, Ghana conference (100+ activists from 82+ language communities)

### Fieldwork and output so far
- **Location:** Naikkhangchori, Bandarban, Bangladesh
- **Recorded:** ~200 minutes of audio, ~1,000 words, ~20 minutes of video
- **On Wikimedia Commons:**
  - Main category: 240 files, including 40 "Chak woman in traditional attire" portraits and about 200 fieldwork photos
  - Audio: 11 long recordings (verbs, numerals 1–100, kinship terms), plus a Swadesh list (140 files) and a Master Elicitation List (323 files)

### Project journey and roadmap
1. **Original plan:** record words from native speakers, transcribe them in IPA or romanised form, publish them on Wiktionary and Wikidata, and build a speech-to-text model and a parallel translation corpus.
2. **The discovery:** the 2016 script exists. Following community practice, the team adopted it.
3. **New possibility:** Chak OCR became a realistic AI goal.
4. **The blocker:** the Chak script is not in Unicode yet. Getting it encoded may take years.
5. **Interim option:** a keyboard in the Unicode Private Use Area (PUA). It has drawbacks:
   - Text only displays on devices that have the keyboard installed.
   - Every document must be converted once the script is officially encoded.
   - Ongoing maintenance needs paid specialists.
6. **Current ask:** pro bono programmers to build the keyboard. The team will then train the community to install and use it.

<style>

  .reel iframe { width: 100%; height: 100%; border: 0; border-radius: 12px; }
